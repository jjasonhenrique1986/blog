I recently took Gallup's strengths assessment — CliftonStrengths. The idea was to better understand where my strengths are concentrated and how they help me in my day to day as a DevOps Engineer. The result surprised me with its accuracy and helped me see more clearly the reasons behind certain decisions, preferences and styles of work and life.

My top five talents were:

- **Discipline**  
- **Learner**  
- **Consistency**  
- **Deliberative**  
- **Intellection**  

In this post, I share how each of these strengths has shown up (and made a difference) in my professional journey.

## Discipline

I like routine, clarity and order. Early in my career, this
seemed like an "organization obsession", but in the DevOps world I found that
discipline is a great ally. Keeping standards in scripts, documenting
processes, automating recurring tasks and structuring environments
in a predictable way are habits that increase teams' confidence and
system stability. This talent also helped me a lot when
studying for certifications.

## Learner

I'm driven by learning. New tools, architectures, practices... they always draw me in. But more than piling up knowledge, I like to put it into practice: studying in order to apply. That pushes me to pursue certifications, as I mentioned in the previous topic, build labs, write on the blog and share what I learn with my coworkers.

## Consistency

I believe clear rules and consistency help build more collaborative environments. This talent shows up when I advocate for good practices everyone can follow, propose improvements that benefit the whole team or work on standardizing delivery processes. That helps avoid favoritism, bottlenecks or arbitrary decisions — things that, in the long run, undermine engineering culture.

## Deliberative

I'm naturally cautious, and that shows in how I make technical
decisions. Before pushing a change to production, I think about risks,
plan the rollback, test hypotheses. I've seen that, in critical
environments, this is a valuable strength. Being deliberate isn't being slow — it's being
strategic. I always think about the worst possible scenario; that helps me
anticipate incidents in the environment.

## Intellection

I like to reflect. To think about how something could be done better, why an incident happened, or how to solve that problem in a sustainable way. This search for depth helps me see beyond the surface and propose more robust solutions. It's also what motivates me to write — because writing forces me to organize my thinking. This talent also stands out in problem solving.

## Final thoughts

Taking CliftonStrengths was more than a self-knowledge exercise — it was a way to validate things that were already present in the way I work. And more importantly: it helped me plan how to use these strengths more intentionally in my professional and personal life.
